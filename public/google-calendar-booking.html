Booking Calls
The Complete Guide

How to replace the “Book Google Meet” button on your website with a real booking system — so a client picks a time that suits them, you get a calendar reminder, and the video call link sorts itself out.

Audience: complete beginner Time needed: about 10 minutes Cost: free, forever

01 Why you need this

Your website currently has a button that promises something it cannot deliver. Let's be precise about what happens.

What the button does right now

On your contact page there is a button called “Launch Google Meet Session”, and a “Book Google Meet” button in the top bar. Both of them send the visitor to this address:

The address your button points to
https://meet.google.com/new
⚠️
This is not a booking link. It is a meeting factory.

That address does one thing: it creates a brand-new, empty meeting inside whoever's Google account just clicked it.

Their browser then shows them a meeting code like qwe-rty-uin. That meeting belongs to them, in their account.

So what do you actually receive?

🚫 What you do NOT get
  • No notification of any kind
  • No calendar entry
  • No email
  • No record that it ever happened
💸 What that costs you
  • The client holds a link you cannot see
  • They must copy it out and message you by hand
  • More effort than just WhatsApping you
  • Looks broken to anyone who tries it
🎁
An analogy that makes it obvious

Imagine your website advertised a dentist appointment and then, when a patient clicked, handed them a blank form and said “fill this in and mail it to us.”

It is technically not broken. But it is not a booking system either, and it makes you look disorganised on a site whose entire purpose is to win paid work.

What a real booking link does instead

Compare the two experiences properly:

🚫
Today — one click, dead end
Client clicks → gets a code → you find out nothing
▼
✅
After this guide — a real booking
Client clicks → sees your free times → picks one → both of you get the same link

02 What you'll end up with

Here is the finished experience, so you know what you are aiming at before you start clicking around in Google.

👤 What the client experiences
  1. Clicks “Book a discovery call” on your site
  2. Sees a simple page with your name and photo
  3. Chooses a date from a calendar
  4. Chooses a time shown in their own timezone
  5. Fills in their name and email
  6. Presses confirm
  7. Gets a confirmation email containing the video link
🧑‍💻 What you experience
  1. Receive an email telling you someone booked
  2. See the call appear in your calendar automatically
  3. Click the link on the event to join when it is time
  4. Never again wonder whether a meeting exists
★
The part that will delight your international clients

Google automatically converts your available times into the visitor's own timezone. A client in London booking your 2pm Lagos slot sees “2:00 PM” as whatever that means to them. You never have to explain timezones, and nobody turns up an hour early or late.

🛡️
You cannot be double-booked

Google reads your real calendar and hides any time you are already busy. A client can never book over something you already have in the diary.

03 Words you'll need

Six terms appear on the Google screens. Here they are in plain English, so nothing you read later is confusing.

Booking page
The public web page a client visits to choose a time. It is a link you share, and Google hosts it for you. This is the thing you are building.
Appointment schedule
Google's name for the whole setup — the page plus the rules about when you are free. You may see this word on the button you click.
Time zone
The region your times are measured in. Yours is Africa/Lagos (WAT, GMT+1). Getting this wrong shifts every slot by an hour.
Recurring / weekly
Repeating every week, rather than being available only in one particular week. Choose this, or your page goes blank after seven days.
Buffer time
Empty space automatically left either side of each booking. Useful so you are not running straight from one call into the next.
Minimum notice
The shortest gap between now and a booking that is allowed. Setting two hours stops someone booking a call that starts in five minutes.
Google Meet
Google's video calling. Choosing it here means a new, private link is made for every single booking — this is the important part.
Confirmation
The automatic email Google sends. It goes to the client, and a copy comes to you. This is how you “know” a meeting exists.

04 Before you start

Five minutes of preparation saves twenty minutes of confusion.

🖥️
You must use a computer, not your phone

Google explicitly does not allow appointment schedules to be created in the mobile Calendar app. If you try on a phone you will not find the option, and you may think something is broken.

Any desktop or laptop browser works — Chrome, Safari, Firefox or Edge. A tablet in landscape is usually fine too.

What you need

  • A Google account — the free personal kind is enough
  • A computer with a web browser
  • About 10 minutes of uninterrupted time
  • Your real working hours, so the page only offers times you can honour
  • A rough idea of how long a discovery call should be (15 minutes is the usual choice)
💳
Does it cost anything?

No. Appointment schedules are free on any Google account. You do not need a paid Google One subscription, and you do not need a business Workspace account.

One limitation to be aware of: a free personal account allows one appointment type only. That is perfectly adequate here — one “discovery call” slot. Paid accounts can add several different lengths.

05 Setting it up, step by step

Ten steps. Each one says what to click, what you should see, and what to do if it does not look like that.

1

Open Google Calendar

1Go to calendar.google.com and sign in with the account you actually use for work.
2You should see your week view — a grid of days and hours.
⚠️
Not seeing your week?

Check you are on the main calendar, not a secondary one. Look at the calendar list on the left and make sure only one is ticked.

2

Find the Create button

1Look for a colourful “Create” button at the top left of the screen. It is a pencil-shaped icon next to a “+”.
2Click it. A small menu of options appears.
3

Choose “Appointment schedule”

1In that menu, click “Appointment schedule”. It is usually the last item in the list.
2Google will show your calendar again, with a new settings panel appearing on the left-hand side.
💡
If you are in the Booking pages section instead

Newer versions of Google sometimes put this under a “Booking pages” heading with a “Create appointment schedule → Add” button. That is the same thing. Either route works.

4

Set how long each call lasts

1Find “Appointment duration” and click the little down-arrow beside it.
2Pick 15 minutes for a discovery call. Choose something longer if you genuinely need more.

Be honest with yourself here. A 15-minute call booked by people who like you is a good thing; a 60-minute slot will sit empty and look like you are hard to book.

5

Set the times you are available

This is the most important step for the client's experience. It is where you say “these are the hours someone can book me.”

1Choose the days you want to take calls. Weekdays are typical.
2Set the hours for those days. Be realistic — only publish times you will reliably honour.
3Check the time zone says Africa/Lagos (or WAT / GMT+1).
4Choose weekly recurring availability, not a single week.
🚫
The mistake almost everyone makes

If you set availability for one specific week only, your booking page will show nothing after that week passes. Clients will land on an empty page and assume you have stopped taking work.

Always pick the recurring weekly option.

6

Click “Next”

1Click the Next button at the bottom of the left panel.
2Google now shows the booking page settings — the part clients will see.
7

Choose the video call option — the key step

1Find “Select how and where to meet” and click it.
2Choose “Google Meet video conferencing”.
✅
This is the setting that makes everything work

Choosing Google Meet means that when somebody books, Google creates a brand-new private video link for that specific booking, puts it in both calendars, and emails it to the client.

This is the exact thing the old meet.google.com/new link could never do.

3Other options on that list are fine for other situations, but avoid them here. Picking “None” means you have to create and send the link yourself, which undoes the whole exercise.
8

Add the details clients will read

1Give it a clear title, such as Free Discovery Call. Anyone with your link sees this, so make it sound appealing.
2Write a short description. Tell them what they will get out of the call and to have their project details ready.
3Check the photo and name shown at the top look right. These come from your Google account.
9

Add the guardrails, then save

These stop you being booked at ridiculous times. Section 06 explains each one in full — a sensible starting point is below.

SettingStart withWhat it prevents
Minimum notice2 hoursSomeone booking a call that starts in five minutes
Buffer between calls5–10 minutesOne call running straight into the next
Max bookings per day3 or 4One client filling your entire week
How far aheadNext 60 daysYour diary being bookable into next year
1Click Save.
10

Get your booking link

This is the address you will publish on your website.

1Look at your calendar grid. Your new appointment schedule now appears as a small panel-shaped marker on the days you made available.
2Click it. A preview opens with an option to open the booking page.
3Open it, then copy the web address from your browser's address bar. It will look something like:
https://calendar.google.com/calendar/u/0/r/appointment/...
🎉
Do the test a stranger would do

Open that link in a private or incognito window — signed out of Google, as if you were a potential client. You should see your name, the available days, and a working time picker.

If it looks right there, it will look right to your clients.

06 Every setting explained

What each option actually does, and what it is worth setting it to.

SettingWhat it meansRecommended
Appointment duration How long each booked slot lasts. 15 minutes for a discovery call
Date & time / Availability The days and hours clients may choose from. Your genuine working hours, in Africa/Lagos
Time zone The region your hours are measured in. Wrong here shifts every slot. Africa/Lagos (WAT, GMT+1)
Recurring Whether the same hours repeat every week. Weekly — never a single week
How and where to meet Whether a video link is created automatically for each booking. Google Meet video conferencing
Minimum notice Shortest allowed gap between booking and the call starting. 2 hours
Buffer between calls Empty space left either side of every booking. 5–10 minutes
Max bookings per day A ceiling on how many people can book you in one day. 3 or 4 for a solo operator
How far ahead How many days into the future clients may book. 60 days
Guest email verification Requires the client to prove they own the email they typed. On — cuts out junk bookings
💡
Why buffer time is worth setting even though it looks wasteful

Without it, someone can book 10:00–10:15 and 10:15–10:30. If the first call runs a minute long, you are late to the second, and a client who is paying you has been kept waiting.

Five minutes costs you very little. It is the difference between looking professional and looking stretched.

07 When a client books

The original question — “how will I know a meeting has been created?” — answered as a timeline.

👤
The client picks a slot and confirms
Some time in the future — perhaps days or weeks away
▼
🔔
You get an email, immediately
This is the answer to your question — you are told, automatically
▼
📅
The call appears in your calendar
With a private video link already attached to the event
▼
✉️
The client gets a confirmation email
Containing the same video link, plus the date and time in their timezone
▼
🔗
On the day, you click the link on the event
It opens Google Meet and you are both in the same room
✓
So: how do you know?

You know because it lands in your inbox and your calendar — without the client needing to tell you anything.

That is the whole difference from the old button, where the information died with the person who clicked it.

Before each call

  • Open your calendar and find the event.
  • Click the Google Meet link on it — this is what puts you in the room.
  • Join a couple of minutes early and check the microphone and camera work.
  • Have their project brief open, because they will have had time to think since booking.

08 Plugging it into your website

You have a working booking page. The last job is making your buttons point at it instead of the dead link.

What is wrong with your site right now

Three separate buttons all point at the same dead address:

  • The “Launch Google Meet Session” button on the contact card
  • The “Book Google Meet” button in the top bar
  • The same button inside the small-screen menu

How they will be fixed

I will add a single setting called BOOKING_URL and point all three buttons at it. This is an ordinary setting, not a secret — you can change your booking page later by editing one value rather than touching any code.

What you will do in the Cloudflare dashboard
Cloudflare  →  Workers & Pages  →  get-tech-solutions
            →  Settings  →  Variables and Secrets  →  Add

Variable name:  BOOKING_URL
Value:          <your booking page link>

Type:           Text        (NOT "Secret")
Environment:    Production
💡
Why a setting rather than hard-coding the link

If you ever switch to Calendly, change your calendar, or want a different page per service, you edit one value in the dashboard. No code change, no redeployment, no waiting on a build.

And if the setting is ever missing, the buttons fall back to your WhatsApp link rather than breaking.

The wording will also change

The current card says “Book a 15-minute discovery session on Google Meet” while doing nothing of the sort. The new wording will describe something that genuinely happens — for example: “Pick a time that suits you. You'll get a confirmation with a private Google Meet link.”

09 If something goes wrong

The five problems people actually hit, and what each one means.

What you seeWhat it meansHow to fix it
No “Appointment schedule” option You are on a phone or tablet, or the Google Calendar app Use a desktop or laptop browser at calendar.google.com
The booking page shows no times Availability was set for one specific week, and that week has passed Edit the schedule and switch to weekly recurring
Times are all shifted by an hour The time zone is set to something other than Lagos Change the time zone field to Africa/Lagos
No video link in the confirmation “How and where to meet” was left as “None” Set it to Google Meet video conferencing and save
A client booked an awkward time Your published hours were broader than your real availability Narrow the hours in the schedule, or cancel the event from your calendar

Changing things later

Your booking page is not frozen. To edit it, open Google Calendar, find the appointment schedule on your grid, click it, and choose to edit. Hours, duration, description and guardrails can all be adjusted whenever your working pattern changes.

Edits apply to future bookings only — anyone already booked keeps their confirmed time.

🔄
Want a more on-brand booking page?

The page is hosted by Google, so visitors briefly see Google's interface rather than your own design. That is the trade-off for having it free with zero maintenance.

If you would rather have a page that matches your site, Calendly (free tier) or Cal.com do the same job with your branding on it. You would swap the BOOKING_URL value and nothing else would need to change.
