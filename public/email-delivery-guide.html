03 The three addresses on an email

Every email carries more than one address, and confusing them is the most common source of misunderstanding here.

📤 From

The address the email claims to be from. Whoever sees the email is shown this name.

Yours: onboarding@resend.dev — Resend's address, not yours.

📥 To

The address that actually receives the email. This is the one that decides whether it is deliverable.

Yours: dammieoptimus@gmail.com — hardcoded in the code.

↩️ Reply-To

Who a reply is routed to when someone hits Reply. This is separate from the From address.

Yours: set to the visitor's email, so your replies reach them.

🛡️ SPF & DKIM

Digital signatures proving the email genuinely came from the From domain and was not forged.

Yours: automatically applied by Resend for verified domains.

Your Reply-To is already correct. When you open an inquiry and hit Reply, it goes to the visitor — so replying to leads works today without any change.

04 Why it works today

The address onboarding@resend.dev is a testing sandbox. Understanding its one rule explains your whole situation.

⚖️
Resend's rule for the sandbox address

You may send from onboarding@resend.dev only if the email is going to your own inbox. Anything else is rejected.

Why that rule never bites you

Your code always sends to dammieoptimus@gmail.com, because the To address is hardcoded. The rule is "sandbox sender is only allowed if the recipient is you" — and the recipient is always you. So the rule is always satisfied, automatically, for every visitor, forever.

🎁
A useful mental model

Think of onboarding@resend.dev as a courier who will happily carry your letters — but only if the return address on the parcel is the courier's own, and only to your own mailbox.

Your parcels are always addressed to your own mailbox. So the courier never complains.

✍️
A correction I owe you

Earlier in this project I warned that this address would "silently fail for real clients." That was wrong. I had not yet accounted for the fact that the recipient is always you.

Your form works for real visitors today. The remaining issues are polish and future-proofing, not defects.

06 Domain verification, in plain English

Three concepts, explained from scratch. No prior knowledge assumed.

What is a domain?

A domain is a web address you own and pay for, such as dammieoptimussolutions.com. You rent it from a registrar, point it at your hosting, and it becomes yours to configure.

⚠️
Your current URL will not work for this

You are currently on get-tech-solutions.dammieoptimus.workers.dev. The workers.dev part belongs to Cloudflare, not you. Cloudflare simply gave you a subdomain for free, so you cannot prove you own it and cannot add DNS records to it. You need your own domain.

What is DNS?

DNS is the internet's phone book. It translates names into addresses. When you add a record in Cloudflare's DNS settings, you are editing your domain's entry in that phone book so the whole world can read it.

Why verification exists at all

Email has no passwords. Without some proof, anyone on the internet could send a message claiming to be yourcompany.com — and your customers would have no way to tell. So email providers require secret codes proving you control a domain before letting you send as it:

DKIM
A digital signature attached to each email proving it genuinely came from your domain and was not tampered with.
SPF
A published list naming which servers are allowed to send email as your domain.
DMARC
Optional extra policy telling receivers what to do when SPF/DKIM fail. Improves inbox placement. Not required to start.
MX
Mail eXchange — points to where mail for your domain is delivered. Only relevant if you also want to receive mail at that domain.

The process, in one sentence

Resend generates the secret codes → you paste them into Cloudflare's DNS → Resend checks they arrived → Resend now trusts you to send as that domain.

🎁
Analogy

It is the difference between a parcel with a handwritten return address — which anyone can write — and one with a government-verified sender ID, which cannot be forged.

Once it is set up it is permanent. You do this once and it keeps working for years.

07 Phase 1 — Buy the domain

Do this first. Everything else depends on it, and it is the step people most often stall on.

1

Choose a registrar

Any of these work. Prices are comparable, roughly $10/year for a .com.

  • Cloudflare Registrar — sells at cost, no markup, but requires the domain to use Cloudflare's DNS (which you want anyway).
  • Namecheap — cheap, wide selection, good interface.
  • Porkbun — cheap, simple, no upsells.
2

Search for dammieoptimussolutions.com

Register it for at least one year. Enable WHOIS privacy if offered — it hides your personal contact details from public lookups at no extra cost.

Add auto-renew so it cannot lapse and break your email sending.

3

Point the domain at your current site

Optional, but strongly recommended — it replaces the long workers.dev URL with a memorable one.

In Cloudflare: Workers & Pages → your project → Settings → Domains & Routes → Add Custom Domain.

Follow the prompted nameserver change at your registrar, then wait for the certificate to issue (minutes to a few hours).

💡
You can do Phase 1 and skip to Phase 2

You do not strictly need the custom domain attached to your site to verify email. If you only want email working, buy the domain and go straight to Phase 2.

08 Phase 2 — Add the domain in Resend

Tell Resend which domain you want to send as, and collect the DNS records it needs.

1

Open Resend → Domains → Add Domain

Log in at resend.com/domains.

2

Enter the domain — prefer a subdomain

Use send.dammieoptimussolutions.com rather than the bare root domain.

Resend explicitly recommends subdomains so that a future newsletter cannot damage the reputation of your important transactional mail. It also sidesteps conflicts if you already have SPF records at the root.

3

Copy the DNS records Resend shows you

Resend will present a short table of records (typically a DKIM record and an SPF record, sometimes a suggested DMARC). You will need each record's type, name, and value.

Copy them somewhere safe before you switch tabs — pasting errors are the single most common cause of a failed verification.

09 Phase 3 — Add the DNS records in Cloudflare

Cloudflare → your domain → DNS → Add record. Add one record at a time, exactly as Resend listed it.

🔴
The one setting people get wrong

If Resend asks for an MX record, you must set it to DNS-only (grey cloud), not proxied.

Orange-cloud proxying is for web traffic. It breaks mail routing completely, and verification will fail for no obvious reason.

Record typeProxy settingWhy
TXT (DKIM & SPF)DNS-onlyText records cannot be proxied anyway. Safe as-is.
CNAME (if offered)DNS-onlyKeep it grey. Some setups work proxied, but DNS-only is the safe default.
MX (if offered)DNS-only — requiredProxying an MX record breaks mail delivery. This is the classic mistake.
📌
A note on SPF records

A domain may only have one SPF record. If you already publish SPF at the root domain, do not add a second one — merge them into a single record instead. Using a subdomain for sending (as recommended in Phase 2) neatly avoids this problem, because the subdomain gets its own separate SPF record.

4

Go back to Resend and click Verify

DNS changes usually propagate within a few minutes, occasionally longer. If verification fails, wait ten minutes and retry — do not re-add the records.

5

Make it the default sending address

Once the domain shows as Verified, set it as the domain's default sender in Resend so anything you send from the dashboard also uses it.

From this moment you can send as any address on that domain — no further setup.

10 Phase 4 — Point the code at your new address

Verification alone changes nothing in your app. One line of code still names the old sandbox sender.

Today — hardcoded

The sender lives directly in the code, so changing it means editing a file and redeploying.

src/app/api/contact/route.ts  ·  around line 44
const emailPayload = {
  from: 'Dammie Optimus Solutions Portfolio Site <onboarding@resend.dev>',
  to: ['dammieoptimus@gmail.com'],
  // ...
};

Better — read it from a variable

Read the address from an environment variable, with a safe fallback to today's value.

src/app/api/contact/route.ts  ·  the change
const fromAddress =
  process.env.CONTACT_FROM ??
  'Dammie Optimus Solutions <onboarding@resend.dev>';

const emailPayload = {
  from: fromAddress,
  to: ['dammieoptimus@gmail.com'],
  // ...
};

Why this is better, in plain terms

  • You set the address once in Cloudflare's settings, not in code.
  • Change your sender later, and there is no code edit and no redeploy.
  • If the setting is ever deleted, the code falls back to the old address instead of crashing.
🔑
Set it where

Cloudflare → Workers & Pages → get-tech-solutions → Settings → Variables and Secrets.

Add CONTACT_FROM with the value Dammie Optimus Solutions <inquiries@send.dammieoptimussolutions.com>.

It is not a secret — no need to mark it Secret. Plain environment variable is fine, and easier to edit later.

11 Phase 5 — Test it end to end

Prove it works before you tell anyone it does.

  • Submit the contact form on your live site with your own details.
  • Confirm the email arrives and the From now shows your own domain.
  • Hit Reply and confirm it goes to the address you typed in.
  • Check the sender is not marked as spam.
  • Submit from a different device or network, to be sure nothing is cached.

You are now unblocked

With a verified domain you can email anyone — not just yourself. Auto-replies to clients, cc'ing the visitor, sending confirmations: all of it now works, with no 403 errors.

12 Environment variables on Cloudflare

The complete reference for this project, so you can rebuild it from scratch if you ever need to.

VariableSecret?WherePurpose
RESEND_API_KEY Yes Worker runtime Authorises Resend to send. Mark as Secret. Never use a NEXT_PUBLIC_ prefix — that would expose it in browser JavaScript.
CONTACT_FROM No Worker runtime The sender address. Optional; falls back to the sandbox address if unset.
NEXTJS_ENV No .dev.vars only Local testing only. Stays in .dev.vars, which is gitignored and never deployed.
🚨
Runtime variables are not build variables

These are separate settings in separate places, and confusing them is the most common cause of "it works but only for me".

Runtime variables must be on the Worker (Settings → Variables and Secrets). Setting them only under a Pages project, or only as a build variable, will not make them available to your live code.

13 Deployment cheat sheet

The commands and settings this project relies on, written down.

Build command (Cloudflare Pages settings)

npx opennextjs-cloudflare build

Deploy command (Cloudflare Pages settings)

npm run deploy

That script expands to:

opennextjs-cloudflare build && opennextjs-cloudflare deploy -- --keep-vars
⚠️
Do not drop the --keep-vars flag

Without it, a deployment deletes any variables you set in the Cloudflare dashboard — including your RESEND_API_KEY. If you ever type the deploy command by hand, include -- --keep-vars yourself.

14 Troubleshooting

Symptoms you might hit, and what they actually mean.

SymptomCauseFix
403 "You can only send testing emails to your own email address" Sending to someone other than the account owner while still using the sandbox sender Verify a domain (Phases 2–4), then set CONTACT_FROM
Emails stopped arriving after a deploy Deploy ran without --keep-vars and wiped your variables Re-add RESEND_API_KEY, then use npm run deploy from now on
Form shows "Email delivery is not configured" RESEND_API_KEY is not set on the Worker at runtime Add it under Settings → Variables and Secrets, apply to Production
Domain stuck "Pending" in Resend An MX record was orange-cloud proxied, or a value was pasted with a typo Set MX to DNS-only (grey cloud); re-check every value character by character
Build fails with "Missing API key" An older build evaluated the email client before the key was available Already fixed — the client is now created per request, so builds no longer depend on the secret
Email lands in spam New domain with no sending history yet Normal for new domains. Send a few real emails over a week; avoid attaching large files

15 Glossary

Every term used in this guide, in one place.

Domain
A web address you own, e.g. dammieoptimussolutions.com.
Registrar
The company you buy a domain from.
DNS
The internet's phone book, translating names into addresses.
Nameserver
The DNS operator for your domain — here, Cloudflare.
DNS-only vs Proxied
Grey vs orange cloud in Cloudflare. Orange proxies web traffic; mail records must be grey.
SPF
Record listing which servers may send as your domain. Only one allowed per domain.
DKIM
Digital signature proving an email genuinely came from your domain.
DMARC
Optional policy telling receivers what to do when SPF/DKIM fail.
MX
Record pointing to where mail for your domain is delivered.
Resend
The email delivery service your code uses.
Sandbox sender
onboarding@resend.dev — for testing only, delivers solely to the account owner's inbox.
Environment variable
A setting stored outside your code, adjustable without editing files.
Runtime variable
An environment variable available to live code when a request is handled.
Worker
Cloudflare's serverless runtime where your app actually executes.
OpenNext
The adapter that lets a Next.js app run on Cloudflare Workers.
Reply-To
Where replies go, independent of the From address.
