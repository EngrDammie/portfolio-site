Android will ask permission to install from your browser — that is the normal step for any app outside the Play Store. The three steps are below.

01

Check it is the file I built

Any download from an unknown source deserves a second look. This one can be checked exactly, because the file has a fingerprint that is the same for every copy and cannot be changed without changing the hash.

After the download finishes, the SHA-256 checksum of TGR_Playbook_base.apk is: 

a83723afd7e087a157c291a464aa1e0268c280d518dde90f9e74a398446a7e9b

The same value on a command line: 

sha256sum TGR_Playbook_base.apk
02

Installing it

  1. Download the file. Tap the button above and let it finish. On mobile data, allow the download to complete rather than switching networks midway.
  2. Tap the downloaded file. Android will say that the source is not allowed. Choose Settings and turn on Allow from this source for your browser or file manager, then go back. This permission is per-app, not global.
  3. Confirm the install. Play Protect may show a warning because the app did not come from the Play Store. That is expected for any app distributed this way. You can review what it is checking and choose to continue.
  4. Open TGR Playbook. There is no sign-up and no login. The manual starts on your phone and stays there, whether or not you have signal.
A straight word about sideloading. Android treats installing from outside the Play Store as a risk, because that is how most malicious apps are distributed too. The difference here is that this file is one I built and published myself, and the checksum above lets you prove that the copy on your phone is exactly the one I published. If you would rather not sideload at all, the web version runs in any browser and does the same job.
